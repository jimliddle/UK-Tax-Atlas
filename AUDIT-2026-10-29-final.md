# UK Household Tax Atlas — consolidated cross-audit
**Date:** 29 October 2026  
**Review:** ChatGPT-6 reconciliation of the earlier ChatGPT-6 audit and Claude's follow-up audit

## Outcome

The two audits agree on the substantial Budget 2025/2026 policy corrections. Claude's follow-up identified further omissions and misclassifications that have mostly been retained. Three remaining issues have now been corrected in the atlas:

1. **Economic Crime (Anti-Money Laundering) Levy, #181.** Claude incorrectly moved the statutory basis to the *Economic Crime (Transparency and Enforcement) Act 2022, Part 3*. The legal charge is in the **Finance Act 2022, Part 3**, with implementing regulations. The atlas now identifies the proper law and reflects the 2026/27 levy bands. See [HMRC guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/pay-your-economic-crime-levy) and [Finance Act 2022](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/3/part/3).
2. **Great British Insulation Scheme, #127.** Its obligation period ended on **31 March 2026**. The entry is now classified as historical/expired rather than a current obligation. See [Ofgem](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/environmental-and-social-schemes/great-british-insulation-scheme).
3. **Statutory Gambling Levy, #262.** Both audits omitted this distinct compulsory levy on qualifying Gambling Commission licensees. It took effect in **April 2025**, separately from gambling excise duties, and funds gambling-harm research, prevention and treatment. It has been added. See [UK Government statutory levy papers](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/statutory-gambling-levy) and [Gambling Act 2005, section 123](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/19/section/123).

**Result:** 263 catalogue records, **254** after excluding the nine entries specifically flagged as cross-references. Neither number should be described as 254 distinct taxes: the catalogue includes fees, thresholds, reliefs, penalties, hypothetical policies and historical items.

## Agreement between the reviews

The following now agree in the live catalogue:

- **Cash ISA restriction (#175)**: Budget 2025's proposed under-65 reduction to £12,000 was withdrawn in Budget 2026; £20,000 retained.
- **Savings and property income tax (#25, #177, #187, #204)**: the October 2026 increase is on top of the earlier two-percentage-point increase scheduled from Budget 2025. The combined increase is **four percentage points**, with devolved distinctions for property income.
- **Vaping Products Duty (#81)** and **Building Safety Levy (#173)**: both correctly recorded as commencing in October 2026, not distant proposals.
- **Domestic electricity VAT (#75, #213)**: the temporary zero rate is **Great Britain-only**; Northern Ireland's domestic electricity remains at 5%.
- **SAF mandate (#116)**: already operational; carbon-price and energy supplier obligations require individual commencement checks rather than generic future labels.
- **Agricultural and Business Property Relief (#37, #38)**, **pension salary sacrifice (#18)**, **pension death-benefit IHT (#22)** and **EV mileage charging (#111)**: clarified rates, limits and effective dates.
- **Diverted Profits Tax (#178)**: historical for the relevant older accounting periods, with Unassessed Transfer Pricing Profits charge succeeding it.
- **Dividend Tax (#24), Remote Gaming Duty (#91), general betting changes (#89), Renewables Obligation (#121)**: updated for measures taking effect in 2026 or scheduled for 2027.
- **Scottish Air Departure Tax (#259), Winter Fuel Payment charge (#258), private-school VAT (#257), low-value import duty reform (#260), Motability VAT/IPT (#261)**: five missing items identified by Claude and retained.
- Several wrong Act, Part or section citations, including those for Bank Levy, Lottery Duty, Machine Games Duty, Domestic Top-up Tax and London Congestion Charge, were corrected in Claude's review and retained.

## Additional checks

Claude flagged the business-rate relief treatment of vape shops/adult gaming centres (#197) and the energy Cost Control Framework (#242) as unverified. Both appear in the published Budget 2026 documents. This confirms that they belong in the catalogue, **not** that every drafting detail or implementation date has been finally enacted.

A remaining methodological issue is statutory versus economic incidence. For example, the **Economic Crime Levy** is legally paid by regulated entities, but there is insufficient evidence to assume that all or any fixed fraction is passed to individual customers. The updated wording avoids claiming definite household pass-through.

## What this audit does not certify

This is a reconciliation of the two published AI-assisted reviews, the current dataset and selected primary sources. It is **not** a line-by-line legal sign-off of all 263 records. In particular:

- Many records still link to a general Budget or legislation landing page, rather than directly to the measure's paragraph or operative section.
- Not every historic Act link or local tariff has been independently checked.
- Some proposed commencement dates, deferred provisions and devolved implementation details may change.
- Cross-reference tagging does not by itself remove all conceptual overlap between tax families and mechanisms.
- No comprehensive browser-based accessibility or cross-device UI test was performed as part of this reconciliation.

The term *final* in this report means it is the **consolidated account of this October 2026 review cycle**, not that future updates or corrections are unnecessary.
